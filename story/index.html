How Far? · a story

The map points the wrong way

Draw a line between two towns on a flat map, and it seems to say which way to go. Does it?

I drew pairs of people from the towns How Far? knows, which hold people, each town as often as it has people. The grey is the pairs, by how far apart they are; the red dot is the middle pair, apart.

Two strangers are a long way apart: only live in the same country, and , almost one pair in three, are more than 10,000 km apart. That is where the map starts to lie.

Chapter 1 · Fine, until it is not

Right up to 6,000 km, then wrong half the time

Chapter 2 · The wrong half of the compass

The map says south, the plane goes north

A line 45° off still heads the right way, roughly. Sometimes the map gets even that wrong: its line leans south and the real route leans north, or the other way round, each by 10° or more. That is of pairs, about one in sixteen, and of those more than 15,000 km apart.

Shanghai to São Paulo is one: on the map, west-southwest; in the air, it leaves heading northwest.

Chapter 3 · Three ways it could be wrong

Not the poles, not China, not this map

Epilogue · Reading the map on How Far?

Trust the orange line

On How Far?, the dashed grey line is the straight one, and the orange one is the shortest route. For a trip of a few thousand kilometres they nearly agree; for the other side of the world, “to set off in” is the one to believe.
